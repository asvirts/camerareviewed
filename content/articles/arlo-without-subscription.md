## What Arlo without subscription still includes

Start with the article Arlo wrote for the day the trial ends, [What options will I have when my Arlo trial ends?](https://kb.arlo.com/000062312/What-options-will-I-have-when-the-Arlo-trial-ends). With no service plan, Arlo says the device continues to have:

- Live streaming
- Push notifications
- Two-way audio
- Auto zoom and tracking
- Local storage recording

The asterisks on that page are messy, and they matter. One note says auto zoom and tracking, and local storage recording, are not available for all devices. The next note says auto zoom and tracking is not available on Essential Pan Tilt cameras, and that those cameras use a feature called Auto Track, which requires a trial or subscription. Read both notes. Do not collapse them into “everything free still works on the camera in the box.”

The US plan article, [What Arlo subscription plans and cloud recording options are available? (United States)](https://kb.arlo.com/000063083/What-Arlo-subscription-plans-and-cloud-recording-options-are-available-United-States), is shorter. It says that after the trial you can subscribe, or you can have no plan and still have live streaming and two-way audio. It points back at the trial-end article for the rest. It does not, in the US text, promise a free cloud archive.

You still need the account. Every storage article tells you to open the Arlo Secure App or log in at my.arlo.com. No plan is not the same thing as no login, no app, or no internet. Live view from another city uses the internet. A notification uses the internet. The clip, if it exists, is a separate question.

### The trial, activity zones, and cloud history

The US plan page says a free one-month trial of Arlo Secure is included for each new Arlo camera, video doorbell, floodlight, or home security system. The trial begins when you finish onboarding that product in the app. The same FAQ says some products from authorized retailers include a plan for a longer stretch. When the trial ends, Arlo’s own “what happens” answer is that you subscribe at my.arlo.com to keep features such as 60-day video history, activity zones, interactive alerts, and 24/7 emergency response.

Activity zones are the paid line people feel first. Without them, the camera can still notice motion and send the push notification the trial-end article keeps. It will not, on that article, let you draw the patch of yard you care about and ignore the rest. Person, animal, vehicle, and package detection are listed on the paid plan page. They are not listed in the no-plan bullet list. This draft does not claim the notification goes silent. It claims Arlo moved the named detections onto the plan.

Video History is the cloud library. Arlo’s plan page calls the paid version 60-day video history, and it says you can record up to 4K on a camera that can do 4K. That library is what disappears with the plan. A live view of the porch right now is not yesterday’s visitor.

### Auto zoom, and the Pan Tilt exception

On cameras that have it, auto zoom and tracking stay in the no-plan list. Essential Pan Tilt cameras are the exception Arlo writes down: Auto Track needs a trial or subscription. If the camera in the room is a pan-tilt, the no-plan feature with a similar name is not the feature you have.

## Arlo local recording without subscription

Local recording is the phrase behind the other searches: arlo local recording without subscription, and the hub that makes it true. Arlo’s setup article, [How do I set up local storage backups using my Arlo SmartHub or Base Station?](https://kb.arlo.com/000062818/How-do-I-set-up-local-storage-backups-using-my-Arlo-SmartHub-or-Base-Station), says most wire-free cameras can record to local storage when they are synced to a SmartHub or base station. Most cameras need that pairing. The article names three that can use a microSD card instead: Arlo Go 2, Arlo Go, and Arlo Q Plus.

The hardware the setup article asks for is specific.

- A SmartHub or base station with a USB port. The VMB5000 has no USB port. It has a microSD slot.
- A USB 2.0 flash drive or external hard drive, Type-A, at least 16 GB free. Network drives are not supported. Drives with more than one partition are not supported.
- Arlo warns you not to let the hub format a drive larger than 2 TB. The hub formats with FAT32, and FAT32’s ceiling is 2 TB. A larger drive comes out of that format at 2 TB.

The switch is called Record Locally, under Storage Settings for that hub. Overwrite Automatically is on by default. When free space on the USB device falls below 20 percent, the oldest recordings are deleted. Files that are not in the Arlo folder are left alone. You can turn overwrite off, and then the app tells you the drive is low so you can enable a different USB device. Only one USB device records at a time. A second stick can sit there for the day the first one fills.

What gets written is narrow. Only automatically triggered recordings go to the USB device or the microSD card. Manual recordings go to the cloud only. The Direct Storage Access article adds snapshots to that cloud-only list, and it says you cannot delete one local clip at a time from the app. Deleting means the drive comes out and goes to a computer.

If the camera and the hub have power and the internet drops, the setup article says the camera keeps recording to the USB device. That is the outage behavior. It is not a promise that your phone, on another network, can see the clip while the ISP is down.

### SmartHub and base station, one model at a time

The compatibility page, updated September 29, 2026, is the list that decides whether your camera and your hub are even a pair. [Arlo SmartHub and Base Station Compatibility](https://kb.arlo.com/000062284/Arlo-SmartHub-and-Base-Station-Compatibility) gives each hub a camera list, a radio, and a storage line.

The VMB5000 takes microSD, microSDHC, or microSDXC, up to class 10 UHS-3 V90. Arlo does not reprint the 16 GB and 2 TB USB numbers for that slot. Its radio is 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz, 300 feet line of sight, with Ethernet. It has no integrated siren. Its camera list is the long one: Ultra and Ultra 2, the 4K Ultra 3rd Gen, Pro through Pro 5, the Pro 2K 6th Gen, Pro 4, Pro 3, the wired floodlight, Essential outdoor cameras including the 3rd Gen, several doorbells, and the older wire-free camera. It does not include the cameras in the “unable to pair” list at the bottom of the page.

The VMB4540 is the same long camera list, with USB Type-A instead of a microSD slot, 2.4 GHz only, and no siren.

The VMB4500 and VMB4000 take USB and have sirens, 90 dB and 100 dB on the compatibility page. Their camera lists are shorter. They include Ultra, Ultra 2, Pro 5S, Pro 4, Pro 3, Pro 2, Pro, the older Essentials, and the doorbells Arlo still lists there. They do not list the Ultra 4K 3rd Gen, the Pro 2K 6th Gen, the Essential 3rd Gen outdoor cameras, or the Essential Solar camera. A new Essential bought in 2025 does not grow a local drive because an old VMB4000 is in the closet. The names Pro 5 and Pro 5S are split across those lists. The lineup page treats Pro 5 and Pro 5S as one discontinued model, VMC4060P. Match the name on the hub page to the name on the box before you assume the pair.

The VMB3500 and VMB3000 say Local storage: No. Their lists are the old set: Pro 2, Pro, the original wire-free camera, and the audio doorbell. If that is the base you still have, the compatibility page does not offer a USB archive.

[How to set up Arlo Cameras](https://kb.arlo.com/000062755/How-to-set-up-Arlo-Cameras) says a hub is required for Pro 3, Ultra, and Ultra 2. Those cameras do not have a skip-the-hub path on that page. Their local archive, if they have one, is the hub they were required to use, and only if that hub’s row says it has storage.

### Watching the clip, at home and away

Two view articles describe two different chores.

[How do I view Arlo videos saved on local storage?](https://kb.arlo.com/1146850/How-do-I-view-Arlo-videos-saved-on-local-storage) says a subscription is not required to use local storage. For the VMB4540 and the VMB5000, it points you at Direct Storage Access. For the VMB4000 and VMB4500, it says you remove the USB device. The files are MP4, in a folder named Arlo, named with the camera serial, a clip number, and a date in YYYYMMDD order.

The newer view article, [the Arlo Secure 4.0 version](https://kb.arlo.com/000062817/How-do-I-view-Arlo-videos-saved-on-local-storage-Arlo-Secure-4-0), says local storage can be used by itself or as a second backup. It repeats the pull-the-drive rule for the VMB4000 and VMB4500, and it says that if your phone is on the same network as the hub you can watch in the app. It also tells you to open Feed and choose Local.

[Direct Storage Access](https://kb.arlo.com/000062997/What-is-Direct-Storage-Access-and-how-do-I-use-it-for-Arlo-video-recordings) is the in-app version, and only for a VMB5000 or VMB4540. Arlo says those recordings never touch the Arlo cloud. The app shows the last 30 days. Older files mean the drive comes out and goes to a computer. There is no thumbnail, because the thumbnail would have been a cloud object. On the same home network, the app can see the hub. Away from home, Arlo recommends a VPN on the router and the phone, and it also names port forwarding as an alternative. This page will not walk through opening a port. A recorder reachable from the whole internet is a different problem from a missing subscription, and it is already covered in [security cameras that work without Wi-Fi](/security-cameras-without-wifi/).

## Arlo video storage without subscription

Arlo video storage without subscription is not one place. People use the phrase for three different things, and Arlo’s articles use it for all three.

The cloud library is Video History. That one requires the plan. The US plan article and the trial-end article agree.

The hub drive is the second place. The view article numbered 1146850 says you do not need Arlo Secure to use it. The setup article calls the same feature a backup, and it says that if you connect a USB device the camera records to the cloud and the USB device. It also says that when the internet is down, recording to the USB device continues.

The card inside a Go 2, a Go, or a Q Plus is the third place. Those cameras are on the list of devices that cannot pair with a hub, so the card is the local path they have, not a hub they cannot join.

### The three sentences that do not agree

Three articles updated September 15, 2026 do not say the same thing about whether the drive replaces the plan.

The enable article, [How do I enable or disable local storage](https://kb.arlo.com/000062816/How-do-I-enable-or-disable-local-storage-for-my-Arlo-SmartHub-or-Base-Station), says all Arlo video recordings are stored to the cloud, and that the USB device or microSD card is a second location. Then it says local storage cannot be used on its own as a substitute for cloud recording.

The 4.0 view article says the opposite shape of that sentence: local storage can be used by itself or as a second backup location.

The trial-end article lists local storage recording among the features you keep with no plan, on the devices that have it.

The Direct Storage Access article says that when you use that feature the recordings never touch the cloud. That is hard to square with “everything is stored to the cloud first,” and it is Arlo’s sentence, not a guess from a forum.

This draft did not cancel a plan and watch a clip land on a drive. It does not know which sentence the app on your phone is obeying this week. If you need cloud history, the USB stick is not that product, because Arlo’s own “cannot substitute” sentence says so. If you need a file in the house after the trial, the trial-end article and the view article both say local storage does not require the plan, on hardware that has it. Believe the article that matches the screen in front of you, and believe a newer edit of either page over this one.

## Arlo Pro 4 without subscription

Arlo Pro 4 without subscription is the case people own. The [Pro 4 FAQ](https://kb.arlo.com/000062512/Arlo-Pro-4-FAQ), also updated September 15, 2026, answers the hardware first. The camera outputs 2560 by 1440. A SmartHub or base station is optional. You can join the router’s Wi-Fi directly. The FAQ’s reasons to add a hub are a separate network, less Wi-Fi traffic, battery life, and local storage. The lens is a 160-degree diagonal. The camera is weather resistant. The magnetic charging cable in the box is for indoor use only. Average upload is about 2 Mbps per camera. The camera includes a trial.

On resolution, the FAQ is the sentence to keep: the trial allows 2K streaming and cloud recording, you can record 2K clips to local storage with a hub or base station, and after the trial you renew the subscription to continue 2K cloud recording. Local 2K and cloud 2K are different lines. The plan renews the cloud line.

### Pro 4 on the router, and Pro 4 on a hub

The [Pro 4 user manual](https://www.arlo.com/content/dam/arlo/support/Arlo%20Pro4_UM%20ENG.pdf) is older than the September 2026 articles. It still says “Arlo Smart” in places. Where it and a 2026 article disagree, re-read the article. Two manual sentences are still the ones the FAQ does not spell out.

The hub is not in the box. Pairing to a VMB5000, VMB4540, VMB4500, or VMB4000 is what the manual says adds 2K local streaming, 2K local recording, auto zoom and tracking, continuous recording with a subscription, local storage on the hub’s card or USB drive, and remote access for the VMB5000 and VMB4540.

If you later move that camera off the hub and onto the router, the manual says you can still cross-trigger a chime or other cameras that remain on the hub. It also says you cannot store that camera’s recordings on the hub’s drive. Direct Wi-Fi is the no-archive setup, once the trial’s cloud history is gone. Moving between the router and the hub, in the manual, means removing the camera from the account and adding it again. That is Arlo’s procedure, not a trick.

The manual’s USB chapter is easy to misread next to the FAQ. It says an active live-stream connection between a Pro 4 and a SmartHub can record to USB at 1080p, and that a 2K local live stream automatically records to USB if a drive is connected. It also says that if the hub is not on the internet, recordings continue onto the USB device. Those are live-stream cases. The FAQ’s “2K clips to local storage” line is the motion-clip case. Keep them apart.

The manual’s format list is wider than the 2026 setup article. The manual lists FAT16, FAT32, exFAT, and the labels ext2FAT, ext3FAT, ext4FAT, and HFS+, a 16 GB minimum, a 2 TB maximum, and USB 2.0, and it says not every hard drive works. The setup article says FAT32, Type-A, at least 16 GB, no network drive, no multi-partition drive, and a warning about formatting anything over 2 TB. Use the list that belongs to the hub you are formatting. A VMB5000 does not use that USB list at all. It uses the microSD slot on the compatibility page.

The lineup page lists the Pro 4 as discontinued, models VMC4041P and VMC4050P, released in 2020. A card on this page is a candidate for a camera you may already own. It is not a claim that Arlo still builds it, and it is not a claim that a used camera was checked.

### Newer Pros and Essentials that still pair

The same split, router or hub, shows up on later cameras. Arlo’s [direct-to-router list](https://kb.arlo.com/000062770/Which-Arlo-Cameras-and-Doorbells-work-directly-with-my-Wi-Fi-router) includes the Pro 2K 6th Gen, Pro 5, Pro 4, the Essential outdoor 3rd Gen, both Essential doorbells, and the cameras that cannot use a hub at all. Direct connection means the camera works without a hub. It does not mean the camera gained a card slot. The setup article’s condition for local storage is still “when synced” to the hub, for most wire-free cameras.

The Pro 2K 6th Gen and the Essential outdoor 3rd Gen are on the VMB5000 and VMB4540 lists, not on the VMB4000 or VMB4500 lists. Arlo doorbells on the compatibility list, including the 2nd Gen video doorbell and the Essential wired and wire-free doorbells, can pair with the hubs that list them. ### Indoor 3rd Gen, Pan Tilt, and the plug-in Essential

The indoor article is the cleanest “this generation lost the hub” page Arlo publishes. [Do I need an Arlo SmartHub for the Essential Indoor Camera?](https://kb.arlo.com/000062559/Do-I-need-an-Arlo-SmartHub-or-Base-Station-to-use-my-Arlo-Essential-Indoor-Camera) says a hub is optional for the 2nd Gen and for the original indoor camera, VMC2040. It says the 3rd Gen is not compatible with SmartHubs or base stations. All of them can join a Wi-Fi router. The 3rd Gen is compatible with continuous video recording, which is the paid always-on cloud feature, not a drive in the house.

The lineup page matches that. Essential Indoor 2K and HD 3rd Gen are VMC3070 and VMC2070, year 2025, 2K or 1080p, AC power, optional PoE adapter, privacy shield. Pan Tilt cameras and the plug-in Essential are in the same unable-to-pair group. They are also on the direct-to-router list, so they function as Wi-Fi cameras. With no plan, the trial-end article leaves them live view, notifications, and two-way audio, and it takes away cloud history and activity zones. ### Go 2, Go, and Q Plus cards

The [Go 2 SD card article](https://kb.arlo.com/000062623/How-do-I-use-an-optional-SD-card-in-my-Arlo-Go-2-Camera) says you can use an optional microSD card, sold separately, to store recordings locally when the Go 2 is not connected to the cloud. The card types are SD, SDHC, and SDXC, UHS-I, class 10, U1, or U3. The same article says local storage cannot be used on its own as a substitute for cloud recording. Both sentences are on the page. The card is for the gap when the camera is off the cloud. Arlo does not call it a replacement for the cloud library.

Watching those files means safely ejecting the card in the app, taking the camera out of the housing, and opening the Arlo folder on a computer. Overwrite is on unless you turn it off. Formatting is an app control on that page.

The lineup lists the Go 2 as VML2030, 1080p, LTE or 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi, with the microSD called out. LTE is a carrier connection. A data plan is not Arlo Secure, and it is not free because the Arlo plan is optional. That distinction is the point of [security cameras that work without Wi-Fi](/security-cameras-without-wifi/). This draft does not quote a carrier price. The Go, the older camera, is discontinued on the lineup page and is named next to the Go 2 for a microSD card.

### Legacy cameras and the 7-day cloud

[End of Life for Arlo Devices and Services](https://kb.arlo.com/000063018/End-of-Life-for-Arlo-Devices-and-Services), updated September 15, 2026, says registered owners of an end-of-life device can still live-stream, get motion notifications, and store clips locally with a compatible hub. A plan still adds cloud storage for supported devices. Firmware updates stop, with critical security updates described for one year past the end-of-life date. Some features will be missing because the device is no longer updated.

The article’s direct question about free 7-day storage is: if you bought a device that included it, you keep it after end of life. It does not, in that US article, print a model-by-model list of who was sold that feature. The US plan article does not print that list either. [What is the new Arlo experience?](https://kb.arlo.com/000062797/What-is-the-new-Arlo-experience) says upgrading to the new app does not require a subscription, and that legacy customers can upgrade without losing free 7-day storage. Check the account. Do not treat a forum list as the US policy.

The hardware dates on the end-of-life page are still useful. On July 1, 2023, Arlo moved the original wire-free camera, VMC3030, and the original Pro, VMC4030, to end of life. On January 1, 2025, it moved Pro 2 (VMC4030P), Q (VMC3040), Q Plus (VMC3040S), Baby (ABC1000), the security light, and the audio doorbell. Q and Baby cannot pair with a hub. Their documented free archive, if the purchase included it, is that 7-day cloud, not a USB drive.

## What Arlo Secure costs in the United States

The dollars live on the plan page, not in the knowledge-base summary. On November 10, 2026, [Arlo’s US plan page](https://us.arlo.com/pages/arlo-secure) listed:

- Smart Aware, one camera: $9.99 a month, or $95.88 a year
- Smart Aware, unlimited cameras: $19.99 a month, or $215.88 a year
- Smart Secure, unlimited cameras: $29.99 a month, or $299.98 a year
- Ultra Secure, unlimited cameras: $49.99 a month, or $479.88 a year

The page says you can save up to $120 a year with an annual plan. The US knowledge-base article describes the same three names and does not repeat the prices. If the page and this paragraph diverge later, the page wins. These figures are a reading of one day.

[Arlo’s September 2026 rename note](https://kb.arlo.com/000063777/Arlo-Subscription-Plan-Changes-September-2026), updated September 17, 2026, says that starting September 15, 2026 the old names moved: Secure Plus became Smart Aware, Secure Premium became Smart Secure, and Secure Premium with 24/7 Recording became Ultra Secure. Arlo says the new features come at no additional cost and that your current subscription price does not change. Someone who subscribed under the old name should not assume the new sticker is their bill. Someone shopping today is looking at the sticker above.

What the tiers add, in the US knowledge-base wording: Smart Aware is single or unlimited cameras, with 60-day video history, Arlo Summary, activity zones, and advanced detection. Smart Secure adds 24/7 professional monitoring, 24/7 emergency response, Arlo Safe, and threat assessment. Ultra Secure adds 30-day 24/7 recording for up to three cameras. The plan page’s feature grid also lists person, animal, vehicle, and package detection, person recognition, and, on the higher tiers, vehicle recognition, custom detection, and audio detections. Footnotes restrict several of those. Person recognition and event captions are unavailable in some regions. Fire detection is a supplement to a smoke alarm, not a replacement. Threat assessment needs the paid plan that includes it, plus emergency response and video verification, and it may be unavailable where you live.

### One address, one plan, and all-day recording

The plan FAQ says one subscription does not cover two addresses. A house and a cabin are two plans. The FAQ also says the best plan for an Arlo Home Security System is Ultra Secure, and footnote 7 says professional monitoring requires Smart Secure or Ultra Secure and the Home Security System, sold separately. A camera plan and an alarm-monitoring plan are not the same checkout.

All-day recording is the Ultra Secure line, and the footnote is strict. 24/7 recording keeps 30 days of continuous history on up to three cameras per paid subscription. Those recordings cannot be downloaded. The cameras have to be plugged into AC power, on a compatible product, with enough upload bandwidth. A battery Pro 4 on a USB stick is not that feature. Local storage, on every article cited here, is triggered clips. It is not a 24-hour file.

The trial is one month unless a retailer bundled more. It starts at onboarding, not at the day the box was shipped. A camera that has been in the account for a year is not sitting on a fresh trial because you read this page.
