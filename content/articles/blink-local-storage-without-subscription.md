## What blink local storage without subscription keeps

Start with [Blink Storage Options](https://support.blinkforhome.com/en_US/using-your-camera/blink-storage-options). The local-storage section says you can save clips without a Blink Subscription Plan. The clip goes to a USB drive in a Sync Module 2, or to a microSD card in a Sync Module XR or Sync Module XR+. You view those clips in the Blink app. They remain until you delete them or the drive is full. When the drive is full, new recordings stop until you free space. Capacity follows the size of the drive you bought.

The same page draws two hard lines. Legacy devices, the XT, XT2, and Indoor (1st Gen), do not support local storage. Sync Module Core does not support local storage. A camera that is eligible on a different module does not become eligible because Core is plugged in next to it.

[Local Storage with Sync Module](https://support.blinkforhome.com/en_US/using-your-sync-module/sync-module-2-local-storage-operation) is the setup article. It repeats the USB path for Sync Module 2 and the microSD path for Sync Module XR. It asks every camera to be on the same Wi-Fi network as the module. For the XR card it asks for a supported format, exFAT recommended, a minimum read and write speed of 10 Mbps, 1 GB to 256 GB, and at least 375 MB free. For the Sync Module 2 USB drive it asks for Type-A, exFAT recommended, 1 GB to 256 GB, and at least 375 MB free. The first insert can prompt a reformat. Blink recommends its own USB drive and its own microSD card. The recommendation is a store link, not a test of other brands.

### The point where Blink calls the drive full

The [Sync Module 2 FAQ](https://support.blinkforhome.com/en_US/faq-sm/sync-module-2-faqs) is stricter than “full.” It says drives larger than 256 GB or smaller than 1 GB are not recognized, and that the drive is considered full when less than 375 MB remains. You get an alert. Nothing more is written. There is no sentence on that FAQ, or on the storage options page, that says the no-plan drive overwrites the oldest clip. Cloud storage is the path with an auto-delete setting. Local storage, on the pages that describe the no-plan mode, waits for you.

The FAQ also says the clips are MP4 and are not encrypted as they are saved. A stick of ordinary video files is easy to play on a computer and easy to carry off. That is a property of the file, stated by Blink, not a lab result. The kit roundup already makes the comparison with bases that advertise encryption. This page only needs the Blink sentence: the USB copy is not encrypted.

The app does not show every file. The FAQ says the clip list displays the first 1,000 local clips from the past 30 days. Older clips are still on the drive. You see them by safely ejecting the drive and opening it on a computer. A 30-day gap in the phone is not proof the clip was deleted.

## Do I need a Blink subscription to use local storage?

No, for the modules and cameras Blink names, and yes the moment a trial or a plan is active. The Sync Module 2 FAQ answers the fee question directly: there are no subscription fees to use the Sync Module 2. Local storage begins saving clips to the USB drive unless you are on a Blink Subscription Plan. On a subscription or a trial, clips save to cloud storage, and eligible cameras get a once-daily backup on the drive. Those backup clips are viewed by moving the drive to a computer. They are not the in-app clip list.

That split is why a new owner can insert a drive and still watch nothing local. The camera shipped with a trial. The trial is a plan for this purpose. The Outdoor 4 FAQ says the camera comes with a 30-day trial of Blink Plus at no cost. Blink’s plan comparison says the free trial begins as soon as you activate the product, and that it does not apply at a location that already has a Blink Plus subscription. Activation is the clock. The day the box arrived is not.

### One Basic plan does not cover the whole module

The Sync Module 2 FAQ separates Plus from Basic. With Plus, all cameras save to the cloud, and a compatible USB drive gets the daily backup. With Basic, one camera saves to the cloud, and the other devices attached to that Sync Module 2 use local storage. The cloud clips follow the auto-delete setting, which that FAQ caps at 60 days, or 30 days in the EU and UK.

Read the paragraph in order. The 60-day auto-delete sentence sits with the subscription behavior. The no-plan local-storage section of the storage options page does not give those clips an age. It says they remain until you delete them or the drive is full. If the app on a Basic account shows a different clock for the cameras that fell back to the USB drive, believe the screen, and then re-read both pages. This draft did not attach a Basic plan to a multi-camera module and watch which clock won.

## Blink video recording without subscription

Blink video recording without subscription means a motion clip on that drive, for a camera the page names. It does not mean a file of the whole day, and it does not mean a saved copy of a live view you started by hand.

The [subscription features page](https://support.blinkforhome.com/en_US/1760895) puts live view recording on the paid list: record a real-time session, and the footnote says that does not apply to extended live views. Photo capture, hourly snapshots compiled into a summary, is on that paid list too. The storage options page’s no-plan local path is the motion clip. A live look at the porch is the no-cost live view. The recording of that look is a different line.

People also ask whether Blink records 24/7. The honest reading of these pages is narrower. They describe motion clips, a live view you open, and, on a plan, hourly stills. They do not describe an NVR that writes every second to a drive in the closet. ## How do I get Blink to use local storage?

Blink’s setup article is the procedure. This draft did not insert a drive. The steps below are the article’s, so you can check them against the app.

The article says every camera has to be on the same Wi-Fi network as the Sync Module. You insert the microSD card in the XR with the label up until it clicks, or you insert the USB drive in the Sync Module 2. The app confirms the insert. Removing the USB drive starts with Local storage, then Safely Eject USB Drive, then the “USB Drive Disconnected” message. Pulling the stick during a local save loses that clip. The FAQ says so.

To confirm the drive, you scroll to the Sync Module status bar on the home screen, tap Local Storage, and look for “USB Drive Connected” or “MicroSD Card Connected.” That screen also shows free space, which is the number that matters once 375 MB is the floor. On a plan, the same screen reports whether the latest clip backup succeeded. A successful backup is not the same status as “this camera is recording locally.”

### The Mini has an extra step

The [Mini FAQ](https://support.blinkforhome.com/en_US/blink-mini-faq) says a Sync Module 2 is required for local storage, and that the Mini must be on the same system as that module. A Mini that created its own system does not join the drive because the module is on the account. The FAQ says to delete the Mini and add it to the Sync Module 2’s system. The Mini can run without any module. That is why the missed step is common. Live view works. The archive never starts.

### Sync Module Core is the module many kits include

The requirements page says Sync Module Core is compatible with all Blink cameras, is not sold separately, and does not support local storage or clip backup. The Outdoor 4 FAQ says the camera is compatible with all Sync Modules, and the US tech specs’ box list is the camera, a mount, a mounting kit, a quick start guide, and two AA batteries. The module is a separate line on the receipt, or it is the one packed in a bundle under a name you have to read.

“A sync module” is not a storage spec. Core satisfies “requires a Sync Module” on the Outdoor 4 FAQ and fails “save clips without a subscription” on the storage page. The original module fails it too, even though a USB port is visible. If the plastic in the drawer has no slot that matches the article you are following, it is not the article’s module.

### Legacy cameras and the 7,200 seconds

The storage options page keeps a separate section for devices and accounts from on or before April 15, 2020. XT, XT2, and Indoor (1st Gen) use cloud storage only. They do not get local storage, clip backup, person detection, vehicle detection, or photo capture. After a trial or a plan ends, what remains is 7,200 seconds of cloud storage per Sync Module, shared by the cameras on that module. You can delete clips to free seconds. Deleted clips are gone. Blink says support cannot restore them.

Legacy accounts, created on or before that date, have the same 7,200 seconds per Sync Module, with a cap of 36,000 seconds per account. Clips also drop when they are 60 days old, if that comes first. Adding a Mini to a legacy account adds 7,200 seconds, shared by the Minis on the account. It does not hand the Mini a private archive.

Live view recording on those old cameras is uneven. The page says XT2 can save a live view recording without a subscription, inside the same 7,200 seconds. XT and Indoor (1st Gen) cannot record a live view even with a plan. A subscription still switches the account to what Blink calls unlimited storage, and canceling returns the devices to the 7,200-second pool.

## What is the difference between Blink cloud storage and local storage?

They are different files, in different places, with different clocks.

No plan, an eligible camera, and a recognized drive: the motion clip is written to the USB drive or the microSD card, it shows in the app within the 1,000-clip and 30-day window, and it stays until you delete it or the drive stops accepting writes.

A plan or a trial, with a drive inserted: the clip is a cloud object. The drive receives a backup about once every 24 hours, for the cameras on the clip-backup asterisk. That asterisk, on the local-storage article, names Mini, Mini 2, Outdoor 4, Wired Floodlight, Video Doorbell, and Indoor and Outdoor (3rd Gen). The backup is not played in the app. The local-storage screen shows whether the last backup worked. You eject the drive to watch the files.

No plan and no drive, or a module that cannot store: there is no motion-clip archive. The storage page’s remainder is live view, motion alerts, and two-way audio.

The features page’s no-cost list is shorter. It names live view, motion-activated notifications, and a manual thumbnail refresh. It does not name two-way audio. The plan blog’s FAQ does: live view, motion alerts, and two-way audio work without a subscription. Three Blink sentences, three shapes. Talk-back is the one to re-check in the app if the features page is the tab you have open. Hearing the camera is inside “real-time video and audio,” which that page does put on the no-cost live view.

### 180 days on one page, 60 days on another

Cloud retention is the same kind of conflict.

The storage options page says a plan includes unlimited cloud storage for motion clips and live views, and then says video cloud storage of up to 180 days for the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom, and 60 days elsewhere. The next bullet says clips delete at the end of that period or at your auto-delete setting, whichever comes first. “Unlimited” on that page is sitting next to a day cap. Read it as “as many clips as you record, until the clock runs out,” because that is what the following sentences say. It is not a promise that a clip from last year is still there.

The [plan features page](https://support.blinkforhome.com/en_US/1760895) repeats the 180-day figure for the United States, Canada, and the United Kingdom.

[Blink’s plan comparison](https://blinkforhome.com/blog/blink-subscription-plans-compared-2026), bylined Oct 26 and filed under a 2026 URL, says cloud video is stored up to 60 days. Footnote 1 says the videos are stored temporarily, up to 60 days from recording, and that a change to the storage duration affects only recordings made after the change.

The Sync Module 2 FAQ caps the auto-delete setting at 60 days, or 30 days in the EU and UK, in the paragraphs about Plus and Basic.

This draft will not pick the number the app is enforcing. The control that matters is the auto-delete setting in the account, read on the day you need the clip. A guide that prints one of those numbers as if the others were typos is how the 60-day and 180-day sentences got flattened into a single fact.

## Blink Mini record without subscription

The Mini FAQ is the page for this question, and it is about the Mini, not every small Blink camera.

The Mini is an indoor plug-in camera: 1080p, night vision, motion detection, two-way audio, powered by 5 volt, 1 amp USB through the included adapter. The cable is about 76 inches. It does not use batteries. It does not need a Sync Module to operate. Blink does not recommend it for motion through glass. It is not an outdoor camera. The FAQ says so in one line.

Cloud storage is the plan. Local storage is a Sync Module 2 with a recognized USB drive, and only when the Mini is not part of a subscription or a trial. The Mini has to be on that module’s system. Shared scheduling is the other reason to add it to an existing system. In the US and Canada it can also be a chime for a Blink Video Doorbell. None of that creates a clip by itself.

Arming still matters. The FAQ says you arm and disarm from the home screen, and that camera settings decide whether clips are saved and which events notify you. A Mini that is disarmed is not a recorder that forgot its subscription. It is a camera you turned off for motion.

Without a plan, live view is the 60-second session, continued by hand up to five minutes. With a plan, the FAQ’s extended session is 90 minutes, and it says that feature is only for Mini. Alexa live view is five minutes by default on that page.

Mini 2 is a different name on the newer pages. The requirements page says Mini 2 does not require a Sync Module to operate, and it includes Mini 2 in the ten-device list. The clip-backup asterisk includes Mini 2 for the plan feature. The Mini FAQ’s delete-and-re-add steps are written for the Mini. This draft did not find a Mini 2 local-storage procedure on the pages fetched November 12, 2026. Do not buy a Sync Module 2 for a Mini 2 on the strength of the Mini FAQ alone. Open the page that uses the name on the box. Room placement for an indoor lens is the [indoor cameras hub](/indoor-cameras/). This page only answers the recording path.

## A Blink camera without subscription

A Blink camera without subscription still has to be a specific camera on a specific module. The general no-cost list, where the pages overlap, is a live view in the app, a motion notification, and an account. The storage page adds two-way audio. The features page does not. Smart detection, Blink Moments, custom snooze, photo capture, live view recording, and the cloud library are the plan, with the model exceptions above.

The network does not become optional. The Sync Module 2 FAQ says Blink devices need an always-on 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi connection, with at least 2 MB per second of upload from the network to the internet. No plan is not an offline camera. A recorder that keeps writing when that radio is dead is the no-Wi-Fi guide, not this one.

The app is not optional either. The FAQ says the Blink app is how you add devices, watch clips, and change settings. An account is the login that app is attached to. Local files on the USB drive can be opened on a computer as MP4, which is the one path that does not need the clip list. Setting the system up still started in the app.

People also ask whether Blink is useless without a subscription. The storage page’s answer is the better one to keep: the camera can still show you the room and tell you something moved. The thing it cannot do, without a plan and without an eligible module and a drive, is hand you yesterday’s clip. Useless depends on whether you needed the clip. If you needed it, the missing piece is the module and the drive, or the plan. It is not a hidden switch.
