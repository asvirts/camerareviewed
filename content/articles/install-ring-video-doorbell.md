Installing a wired Ring video doorbell is a low-voltage electrical job plus a Wi-Fi setup. Ring’s own hardwiring page opens with the hazard, not the lifestyle photo: disconnect power at the breaker or fuse box, use caution with the wires, and remember that a qualified electrician and a permit may be required where you live. This draft follows that page. It is not a record of a doorbell we mounted, and it is not a substitute for the manual that shipped with the model you bought.

If the question in your head is the monthly fee, read [Do you need a Ring subscription?](/ring-subscription/) before you drill. Power and billing are unrelated. A perfectly wired doorbell still will not keep yesterday’s video without a plan, according to Ring.

## Stop if any of these are true

Stop and hire a qualified electrician if you cannot find the breaker that kills the doorbell, if the transformer label is missing or outside the range for your model, if the wires are damaged, or if you are looking at anything that might be mains voltage rather than a doorbell circuit. Doorbell circuits are low voltage when they are doorbell circuits. People get hurt by assuming a pair of thin wires could not possibly be something else. Ring also says incompatible power sources can damage the device and cause electric shock. Lighting transformers are the example they are warning you off.

Stop if you rent and you have not asked. A doorbell is attached to someone else’s wall and someone else’s chime. Permission is not a step in the Ring PDF and it is still a step.

Stop if the porch has no Wi-Fi and you thought “wired” meant “no network.” It does not. [Security cameras that work without Wi-Fi](/security-cameras-without-wifi/) explains the difference. A PoE doorbell is a cable back to a switch. A transformer-powered Ring doorbell is still a Wi-Fi client.

## Name the model before you touch a wire

Ring has renamed products. The hardwiring article says so at the top and points at a name-change list. The voltage table is where the rename bites. “Wired Doorbell Pro (formerly Video Doorbell Pro 2)” is not the same row as a first-generation Video Doorbell. Using the 8-volt end of an old table on a model that wants 16 volts is how hardware gets returned.

### Transformer ranges Ring published

As published on Ring’s hardwiring guidelines, these are the transformer ranges for an existing doorbell system:

Battery doorbells and Video Doorbell Elite: 8 to 24 VAC, 50/60 Hz, 5 VA to 40 VA.

Video Doorbell Wired: 10 to 24 VAC, 50/60 Hz, 8 VA to 40 VA.

Wired Doorbell Plus, Wired Doorbell Pro (formerly Video Doorbell Pro 2), and Wired Doorbell 2K Plus: 16 to 24 VAC, 50/60 Hz, 10 VA to 40 VA.

Wired Doorbell 4K Pro: 16 to 24 VAC, 50/60 Hz, 10 VA to 40 VA.

The comparison table on this page repeats those rows so the differences are visible. They are Ring’s figures, not measurements we took. If Ring edits the article, Ring’s article wins and this page is stale until the updated date moves.

Ring also sells accessories it says are designed for this job, including DIN rail transformers and a plug-in adapter at 24 VDC, 500 mA. The plug-in adapter is for specific conversions. Ring says it is not compatible with Peephole Cam or Video Doorbell Elite. Newer doorbells may ship with their own DC supplies. Do not replace that supply with a generic adapter because the plug fits.

## Chimes, diodes, and the models that will not use your chime

### Diodes and chimes that will not ring

Ring’s compatibility table is the second place people skip. For an existing mechanical or digital chime, some older doorbells need a diode on a digital chime and some do not. The article’s table marks diode “yes” for Video Doorbell (1st gen) and Video Doorbell 2, and “no” for a long list of later battery and wired models. Read your row. A diode installed because a forum post from another model said so is not careful.

The important exception is newer and easy to miss. Ring says Wired Doorbell 4K Pro and Wired Doorbell 2K Plus are not compatible with existing chimes, unless the 4K Pro is powered by PoE. If you want a sound inside the house, Ring points those models at a Chime Kit Mini. If you skip that sentence, you will wire a doorbell that powers up and never rings the box in the hallway, then blame the transformer.

Connecting directly to an AC transformer, rather than through a chime, has its own table. Ring marks a Pro Power Kit as required for Wired Doorbell Plus and Wired Doorbell Pro, and not required for Wired Doorbell 4K Pro or the battery models. A resistor is marked required for some early Video Doorbells and not for later ones. Buy the kit your row names. Do not improvise a part because the shape looks close.

## The order of work, at the level Ring’s warning supports

This is a sequence, not a torque spec and not a photo essay. The model’s manual has the screws and the bracket pictures. Follow that manual for the hardware. Use this sequence so you do not skip the hazard.

1. Confirm the exact model name against Ring’s current name list and against the transformer table. If you cannot, you are not ready to cut power and guess.
2. Find the transformer. It may be near the chime, in a basement, or in a closet. Read the label. If the voltage or VA is outside the row, do not “try it and see.”
3. Turn off the breaker that feeds the transformer. Confirm the old doorbell is dead by pressing it. If it still rings, you turned off the wrong breaker.
4. Remove the old button only after the circuit is dead. Note which wires were on it. You are reconnecting a low-voltage pair, not inventing a new circuit. If the wires are too short to work safely, that is an electrician’s extension, not a twist you hide in the wall with tape you had in a drawer.
5. Mount the bracket the way the manual shows, with the doorbell’s wedge if you need the lens to see a face rather than the sky. Height and angle are in the manual and in Ring’s separate placement notes. A doorbell that stares at the ceiling is a successful electrical install and a useless camera.
6. Attach the wires to the new doorbell as the manual shows for your path: through the chime, or direct, with the power kit if your row requires it. Restore power only when the connections are finished and the wires are not pinched in the bracket.
7. Join the doorbell to Wi-Fi in the Ring app. This is the step that fails in houses where the router is at the far end and the porch is a dead zone. If the app cannot hold the network, no amount of correct voltage will produce a live view. Move a mesh node or accept a different design.
8. Test the indoor sound you actually wanted. Chime, Chime Kit Mini, or app-only. Do not discover the 4K Pro chime exception after the guests arrive.
9. Decide about the plan during the trial, using the subscription guide. The wires will not save the clip for you.

## After it powers on

Live video and the answer button are available without Protect, as Ring describes on the plan pages. History is not. Set a reminder for the end of the trial so you do not lose the clips you meant to judge. Shared users may not be able to purchase the plan. The account that owns the device does.

Firmware and the Wi-Fi password are now part of the doorbell. When you change the wireless password, the doorbell falls off until you update it. That looks like a wiring failure and is not one. Check the network before you pull the bracket off again.

If you wanted a doorbell that stores video on a device in the house with no plan, Ring’s published model is the wrong target. ## What this page will not do

It will not give you a wire color and tell you it is universal. Colors vary. The old button is the map.

It will not tell you to work live. Ring’s caution is the opposite.

It will not estimate a “typical” install time or a “typical” savings versus a paid installer. Ring has sold professional installation as an add-on, with its own price on the product pages, and that price is Ring’s to quote. If you want that service, buy it from them and let them own the result.
