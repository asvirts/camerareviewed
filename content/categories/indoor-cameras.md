Indoor cameras are easier to power and harder to justify. A plug is usually nearby. Weather is not the problem. The problem is pointing a microphone and a lens at a room where people live, work, or take care of children. A buying page that skips that is not finished.

This hub is the indoor shelf of CameraReviewed. The first edition does not yet have a standalone indoor roundup. The decision framework still applies, and it lives in [security cameras without a subscription](/security-cameras-without-subscription/). Read that before you shop an indoor model on the strength of a “no fee” badge. An indoor camera with a microSD slot can record locally. It can also upload clips, and it can still require an account before the app will show you the card.

## Constraints that matter indoors

Power is usually USB or a wall plug. That sounds minor until the only outlet is behind a couch and the cable crosses a walkway. Battery indoor cameras exist and then become another device you forget to charge.

Storage should be boring. A card in the camera is fine for a single room if you accept that anyone who takes the camera takes the footage. A small hub on a shelf can hold several cameras and stay inside. A full NVR is usually more system than one indoor camera needs, unless the indoor units are part of a PoE set that also covers the yard. That system question is in [security cameras that work without Wi-Fi](/security-cameras-without-wifi/).

Privacy is not a footnote. Tell the people who use the room. Do not put a camera in a bathroom or a bedroom and call it security. Do not use this site as instructions for hiding a camera. Firmware updates still matter on a camera that never goes outside; the network path is the same.

Arlo’s Essential Indoor Camera (3rd Gen) is called out on Arlo’s compatibility page as unable to pair with a SmartHub. The 2nd Gen still can. That limit is explained in [Arlo without subscription](/arlo-without-subscription/). It is not an indoor buying roundup.
